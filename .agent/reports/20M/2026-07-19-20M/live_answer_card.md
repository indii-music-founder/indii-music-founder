## What does it do?

**Short answer:** indii is a music-business operating system for independent artists and their teams. It connects creative work, audio and release preparation, rights and publishing, finance, marketing, and specialized assistants in one music-native workspace.

**Deep answer:** An independent music business currently moves the same song, artist identity, metadata, rights, money, creative assets, and campaign decisions through disconnected tools. indii is designed to keep that context connected across the lifecycle. The repository contains substantial implementation across those areas. A fresh post-merge audit must still distinguish what is implemented, locally reproduced, production-proven, and externally accepted.

## Why do I need it?

**Short answer:** Running a music business means forcing generic tools and disconnected music services to understand the same artist, release, rights, and money repeatedly. indii is the round peg for that round hole.

**Deep answer:** The customer is paying to reduce duplicate setup, data entry, context switching, brittle integrations, handoff failures, and music-specific mistakes—not merely to cancel subscriptions. Accounting software does not naturally understand releases, splits, royalties, or recoupment; a design tool does not carry rights and release context; a distributor does not run the rest of the company. Real cohorts must still measure time saved, errors avoided, displaced subscriptions, and improved outcomes.

## What problem does it solve?

**Short answer:** Fragmentation. Artists are expected to operate like companies, but their tools behave like unrelated departments without a shared source of truth.

**Deep answer:** indii is designed to connect the master recording, metadata, rights, money, creative assets, decisions, and specialist guidance so the artist does not remain the human integration layer. High-stakes actions remain subject to evidence and approval gates.

## Who is it for?

**Short answer:** Serious independent artists and small teams releasing repeatedly and treating their catalog as a business.

**Deep answer:** The first customer has enough activity to feel the pain of disconnected creative, release, rights, finance, and promotion workflows, but not enough staff to build a label back office. It is intentionally music-specific, not the operating system for a flower shop.

## Why not use QuickBooks, Canva, a distributor, and AI chat?

**Short answer:** Those are good point tools, but the customer still has to integrate them. indii's value is shared music-business context and a connected workflow.

**Deep answer:** General tools are optimized for separate jobs. The customer repeatedly re-enters context, reconciles output, and decides what each tool needs to know. `/20M` separates gross stack price, credibly displaced cost, vertical-fit value, portability, and cost-to-serve rather than pretending every stack dollar becomes willingness to pay.

## Why now?

**Short answer:** Independent artists already run complex digital businesses. AI makes specialist help more accessible while making trustworthy context, records, and approval gates more important.

**Deep answer:** More creation and automation increase the number of assets, decisions, rights, campaigns, and costs that must remain coherent. The opportunity is not another chatbot; it is specialized intelligence combined with deterministic business records and human approval inside the artist's workflow.

## Why can this win?

**Short answer:** If indii proves one music-native workflow saves time, reduces errors, and helps artists complete valuable work, its connected context becomes more useful and harder to replace than any individual feature.

**Deep answer:** Code breadth is not the moat. Durable advantage requires retained customers, trusted catalog/rights/money lineage, clean data and IP rights, verified partner boundaries, measured outcomes, and a team that can operate beyond the founder.

## Why invest?

**Short answer:** This is presently an early product-and-execution thesis, not a mature traction thesis: an unusually broad integrated head start that can become a music-native operating platform if the flagship workflow and retained paying cohort are proven.

**Deep answer:** Capital should buy value-inflecting evidence: a secure independently reproduced journey, arm's-length paying users with retention and cost-to-serve data, and clean partner/IP/operational transferability. Those proofs change the company from a large working product into an evidence-backed business.

## What does $20M mean?

**Short answer:** It is the north star, not today's valuation. The central math is about $4M–$5M of quality ARR at 4×–5×, plus buyer-grade retention, margin, controls, and transferability.

**Deep answer:** At 4× ARR, $20M EV requires $5M ARR: about 8,504 customers at $49, 5,275 at $79, or 4,209 at $99 before churn and discounts. The same system accepts $50M, $100M, $1B, or custom targets without weakening the evidence bar.
