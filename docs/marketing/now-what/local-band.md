## Educational article — ready for editorial review

# You’re a popular local band. Now what?

Finishing one milestone creates a new business question. You do not need to solve your whole career today. Start with a small inventory of where you are and decide which task deserves attention first.

## Make yourselves easy to book

Gather a short biography, live footage, listening links, and one booking contact in an EPK. Keep the details somewhere you can revisit and share with the people working on the project. Mark what you know and what still needs confirmation.

## Know the show economics

Write down travel, rehearsal, merchandise, and performance costs before agreeing a show budget. Separate completed work from assumptions. An account, an upload, or a promise from a service does not by itself confirm every part of your music business is covered.

## Build the next-market plan

Identify one nearby market and research suitable venues. Agree show terms in writing. Give that task a clear owner and completion date. Then review the result before deciding what comes next.

Artist Passport asks about your stage, rights setup, distribution, and priorities, and provides an initial business roadmap. Its guidance is based on your answers; it does not independently verify registrations, rights, earnings, or eligibility.
