# Fan social commerce and numbered song editions — concept record

**Recorded:** October 7, 2026
**Source:** Founder concept conversation and the supplied “Limited-Edition Song Drops & Fan Store” developer brief.
**Status:** Future-work concept, not an approved design, launch claim, or implementation mandate. The [competition dossier](../funding/COMPETITION_AND_NOVELTY_DOSSIER.md) tracks market evidence separately.

## The connected experience

An artist has a personal, expressive page and store; a fan has a MySpace-like page or hub centered on favorite artists. A fan-controlled widget connects that fan to each chosen artist's music, news, merchandise, and store. “Install a widget” could mean an indii profile component, an embeddable web widget, or an OS/browser widget; the intended surface and permissions remain open. Discovery, follows, community, and direct artist–fan relationships are part of the concept. The artist store could offer print-on-demand merchandise, unique downloadable items, STL files for fan printing, and numbered song editions. The store is one part of the larger artist business workspace, not a separate, disconnected marketplace.

The emotional model for a numbered song edition is a small vinyl pressing: fixed supply (for example, 500 or 1,000), a serial number for each copy, an artist–fan story around the release, and an initial sale followed by possible fan-to-fan transfers. A resale share could return value to the artist if a real secondary market develops. Appreciation is speculative and must never be promised. The broader “1,000 true fans” thesis requires a useful ladder of offerings and repeat purchases; 1,000 copies at $5 yield $5,000 gross before fees and costs, not a sustainable income by themselves.

## Requirements and hypotheses to preserve

| Area | Founder intent / proposed direction | Decision or proof still needed |
|---|---|---|
| Edition | Artist sets an immutable maximum supply at release; each genuine copy is #N of M. A drop should feel like an event. | Define issuance, failed/refunded sales, reserved inventory, fraud, transfers, and what “immutable” means contractually and operationally. |
| Audio | Lossless WAV-quality master, with 24-bit/96 kHz acceptable; roughly 6–7 minute track target, with longer works optionally split into Part A/B. | Confirm actual artist/DJ use cases, storage/delivery costs, format compatibility, accessibility, and whether the length cap is a product rule or guidance. |
| Ownership and resale | Buyers may resell or trade verified copies; an artist receives a stated share of platform-mediated resale. | Confirm demand, fee economics, payout and refund handling, off-platform transfers, and how rights to listen/download differ from registry ownership. No system can guarantee a share of every exchange of an openly downloaded file. |
| Copy resistance | Discourage casual sharing and make leaks attributable. The supplied brief proposes an encrypted master, ownership registry, app player/unwrapper, key revocation/reissue, and per-copy forensic watermarking. | Choose locked playback, open lossless file with watermark (brief's preferred starting hypothesis), or hybrid with an exit download. Test watermark robustness and DJ compatibility. Revocation cannot retract a plain file already downloaded. |
| Provenance | An indii-controlled registry could record edition, supply, copy, owner, transaction history, and royalty terms. Optional on-chain anchoring is a separate decision. | Model privacy, auditability, portability, recovery, disputes, shutdown export, and third-party verification. Avoid assuming blockchain makes resale royalties enforceable. |
| STL downloads | Fans may buy files to print personally; artist may offer commercial-print rights separately. | Define license grant, permitted quantity/uses, derivative rights, artist upload warranties, infringement handling, versioning, and buyer delivery. File copying remains possible. |
| Merchandise | Print-on-demand goods can sit beside digital files and song editions. Artist chooses provider; no inventory is the concept. | Confirm provider integration, fulfillment/returns, catalog sync, margins, and who is seller of record. |
| Fan widget | A fan curates favorite artists and reaches their updates, music and offers from one place. | Decide placement, embed/privacy/security model, moderation, user consent, notification cadence, and whether it reflects follows or a separate collection. |
| Business model | Primary sales, repeat drops, merch, STL, and conditional resale revenue form a portfolio. | Model gross-to-net economics, platform/processor fees, tax, artist payouts, churn, and whether any collector/DJ behavior exists. |

## Repository truth at intake

The current renderer has social profiles/follows and posts with product attachments (`SocialFeed.tsx`, `UserProfileHeader.tsx`), a marketplace storefront and purchase flow (`MarketplaceStorefront.tsx`, `MarketplaceService.ts`, `createMarketplaceCheckout.ts`), and a signed-download path for purchased stem packs. `LimitedDropService.ts` creates owner-scoped **draft campaigns** with notification setup required. These are useful foundations, but none proves an issued numbered audio edition, resale and artist split, open-file watermark, STL fulfillment, or the proposed favorite-artist widget. Existing generic product types must not be confused with verified fulfillment for each type; the historical ISSUE-1155 closure is a recorded resolution, not proof of this new end-to-end concept. The older [social drops implementation document](SOCIAL_DROPS_IMPLEMENTATION.md) predates some code and is not the present-state authority.

No feature was implemented or GitHub issue created from this conversation. If this advances beyond concept, the smallest meaningful vertical proof is: artist defines a fixed edition with rights and terms → fan finds it through artist/social context → real payment creates exactly one numbered entitlement → the buyer receives an authorized file and can see #N/M → refund/recovery behavior is verified. Resale, watermarking, widgets, and richer social surfaces need their own evidence before they become acceptance criteria. This is sequencing advice, not an approved build.

## Competitive and novelty discipline

The concept must be differentiated by demonstrated user value and evidence, not by claiming indii invented limited music editions, lossless downloads, fan communities, or widgets. A [Sound release page](https://www.sound.xyz/mickeyvalen/meet-me-feat-noe) documents numbered music editions and lossless owner downloads; its present service status needs separate verification. [Bandcamp](https://bandcamp.com/artists) offers high-quality downloads, merch, artist–fan relationships, and fan data; its [guide](https://bandcamp.com/guide) documents embeddable players. [Catalog's closing notice](https://fin.catalog.works/) illustrates continuity and portability risk. Royal's [current site](https://royal.io/) does not establish the status of its earlier music-rights offering. The supplied brief's claims that Sound or Royal shut down, that Bandcamp lacks a fan/social layer or widget, and that all creator-resale fees are enforceable or unenforceable are **unverified or overbroad** and should not enter investor language as facts.

The testable indii hypothesis is a connected, artist-authorized path from rights and catalog state to edition terms, fan relationship, transaction, entitlement, portability, and attributable income, with evidence retained in the Artist Business and Execution Graphs. This combination is a proposed distinction, not proven novelty or a claim that no competitor offers it. Review the [dossier](../funding/COMPETITION_AND_NOVELTY_DOSSIER.md) before using any external comparison.

## Gates and adjacent work

Counsel and payment/tax specialists must review ownership of masters/compositions/samples, resale marketing and investment-language risk, digital licensing, seller of record, taxes, KYC/payouts, and shutdown obligations before a paid launch. The product team must decide the primary buyer (collector, DJ, or artist seeking durable income), playback/file policy, registry portability, edition and pricing rules, resale share and platform fee, watermark approach, and how this fan economy fits the artist-workspace story. A reported slow/client-rendered landing page and weak metadata response in the supplied brief is an **unverified separate audit candidate**; inspect source, Search Console, and mobile performance before filing or changing it.
