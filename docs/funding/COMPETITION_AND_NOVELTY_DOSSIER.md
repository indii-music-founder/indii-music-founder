# indii.music Competition & Novelty Dossier

**Evidence snapshot:** October 5, 2026
**Purpose:** Investor-ready map of adjacent products, overlaps, evidence-backed distinctions, and remaining product proof. This is a dated desk-research snapshot, not a complete market census, legal novelty opinion, or proof of customer demand for indii.music.

## Executive position

The reviewed market includes capable products in royalty recovery, publishing administration, distribution, artist operations, fan CRM, direct-to-fan commerce, rights-holder operations, and identity enforcement. Several overlap with individual parts of indii.music; Mogul and Notes.fm are especially relevant to rights, registration, publishing, and royalty collection. Aumio reinforces the distribution-centered artist-assistant direction, while Laylo shows agentic fan-commerce positioning. The evidence does not justify claiming that indii has no competitors or that any single competitor lacks useful capabilities.

The strategic thesis is a connected **Artist Business Graph**—identity, works, recordings, ownership, authorization, registration, delivery, usage, claims, royalties, accounting, fans, and commerce—connected to an **Execution Graph** that preserves source evidence, decisions, dependencies, bounded execution, verification, terminal outcomes, and learning. Repository evidence supports parts of this architecture; it does not establish a finished or commercially validated end-to-end product. In the products reviewed through October 5, 2026, we have not identified another product combining this same stated scope. That is a bounded research observation, not an exclusivity claim.

## Novelty, evidence, and what remains to prove

The defensible distinction is the **intended connection** between artist/business entities and execution evidence. Repository review on October 4–5, 2026 found a persisted execution-graph foundation with deterministic identity, provenance, relations, idempotent intake, and evidence-gated completion; existing music entities, rights, distribution, commerce, and workflow components also exist. The Rights & Royalty Recovery graph is not yet proven as one live, end-to-end user workflow. Provider registration work is marked `manual_required` in the inspected baseline, and no real customer recovery outcome is established here.

The separate axes must remain separate: creation provenance ≠ identity ≠ voice/persona authorization ≠ ownership ≠ distribution authorization ≠ impersonation. JEV/TypeSafe may help classify ambiguous evidence, but deterministic repository/provider evidence and human authority govern identity, status, dependencies, and completion.

For diligence, use this bounded formulation: **“Among the products reviewed through October 5, 2026, we have not identified another product combining the same specific set of canonical artist-business state, rights/registration/recovery relationships, cross-domain execution, and evidence-gated completion.”** This describes the scope of a review, not patentability, freedom to operate, or absence of competitors.

The highest-value validation is to demonstrate genuine artist workflows: identify a real catalog discrepancy, establish the underlying work/recording/ownership/authorization evidence, execute or guide a provider-accepted correction/registration, reconcile a real statement/payment, and retain verifiable evidence. For commerce, demonstrate an authorized, attributable event-to-action-to-revenue path. For identity abuse, demonstrate provenance and authorization evidence across providers without collapsing the distinct concepts above. These are validation criteria, not work authorized by this market note alone.
