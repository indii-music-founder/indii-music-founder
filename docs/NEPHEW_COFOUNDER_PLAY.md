# The Nephew Play: Removing Terminal Risk via Technical Co-Founder

**Date:** 2026-05-10  
**Context:** Terminal valuation risk is single-author concentration (William Roberts = 96.3% of commits). Acquiring requires knowledge transfer guarantee. Nephew has credible engineering background + clout, but no prior involvement in the project.

**The Problem:**
- Current valuation: $500-700K (capped by bus-factor-1 risk)
- Acquirers need: credible technical co-founder to own knowledge transfer + retention post-deal
- William's constraint: $25K Founders Pass revenue is personal runway (rent, living expenses), not discretionary hiring budget

---

## Five Paths to Nephew Co-Founder Commitment

### Option 1: Nephew Does Spec Work (Equity + Post-Deal Payout)

**Mechanics:**
- Nephew commits 2-3 weeks to code audit (no upfront cash)
- Audits critical systems: DDEX rail, Stripe integration, agent stream, Firebase architecture
- Documents his findings + commits to 12-month post-acquisition retention
- Gets: Co-founder equity + $X bonus if acquisition closes

**Pros:**
- No cash outlay from William's runway
- Nephew's skin in game = credible commitment
- Lower risk if deal doesn't happen

**Cons:**
- Nephew takes risk (2-3 weeks work, paid only if deal closes)
- Works only if nephew trusts you + believes in the deal
- Requires him to believe equity is worth something

**Best for:** Nephew who's genuinely excited about the project + has cash reserves to absorb the risk.

---

### Option 2: Founders Fund the Nephew Involvement

**Mechanics:**
- One of the 10 Founders Pass customers pays additional $5-10K for technical audit/co-founder involvement
- Pitch: "This de-risks your $2500 investment. We're bringing in a credible engineer to ensure technical continuity."
- Nephew gets paid directly by the founder
- William's runway stays intact

**Pros:**
- William's cash flow untouched
- Founder sees it as protecting their investment
- Nephew gets paid in cash (no risk)
- Creates alignment: founder is invested in success

**Cons:**
- Requires founder buy-in (not all will)
- Adds complexity (triple-party agreement)
- Might feel awkward to ask

**Best for:** If one of the 10 founders is particularly engaged + has budget.

---

### Option 3: Defer It (Keep $25K as Runway, Address Later)

**Mechanics:**
- Use $25K to stabilize your life for 3-6 months
- Keep the terminal risk on the valuation for now
- If traction continues, allocate future revenue to pay nephew
- Revisit when you have cleaner cash flow

**Pros:**
- Solves immediate survival problem
- Buys time for traction to de-risk the company itself
- No pressure on nephew or founders

**Cons:**
- Terminal risk stays capped at $500-700K valuation
- Delays acquisition conversations by months
- If momentum stalls, never get to nephew phase

**Best for:** If you're confident traction will keep coming + can wait 3-6 months.

---

### Option 4: Small Angel Round to Fund Nephew + Runway

**Mechanics:**
- Raise $15-25K seed specifically for:
  - $5-10K to pay nephew for 2-3 week code audit + co-founder commitment
  - $10-15K to top up runway (so his $25K isn't your only lifeline)
- Frame to angels: "Removing terminal risk before acquisition conversations."
- Nephew gets paid in cash, commits post-deal

**Pros:**
- Nephew gets real money (removes hesitation)
- Your runway is de-risked
- Cleaner path to acquisition conversations
- Shows investor confidence in the deal

**Cons:**
- Requires fundraising effort (emails, pitches, diligence)
- Dilutes cap table (small amount, but still)
- Adds pressure to close acquisition to justify the raise

**Best for:** If you're comfortable with fundraising + confident in acquisition path.

---

### Option 5: Nephew Commits in Principle (No Upfront Audit, Verbal Commitment)

**Mechanics:**
- Get nephew to agree verbally + email that he'll:
  - Review code post-LOI (if acquisition moves forward)
  - Commit to 12-month knowledge transfer post-close
- Use his name/résumé + commitment in early acquisition conversations
- "Our technical co-founder [Nephew] is already committed to retention."
- Full code audit happens only if deal advances to diligence

**Pros:**
- Zero upfront cost/risk
- Enough credibility to move initial conversations forward
- If deal doesn't happen, no wasted time
- Nephew commitment is "if deal happens" not "forever"

**Cons:**
- Less credible than full audit (acquirer might ask "did he really review it?")
- Nephew needs to be comfortable being named publicly
- Acquirer might demand full audit anyway during diligence

**Best for:** Getting initial acquisition conversations started without full commitment from anyone.

---

## Recommendation

**Rank by likelihood of working:**

1. **Option 4 (Small Angel)** — Cleanest, removes all constraints, signals confidence
2. **Option 1 (Spec Equity)** — Works if nephew trusts you + deal is real
3. **Option 2 (Founder Funds)** — Works if a founder is engaged enough to fund
4. **Option 5 (Verbal Commitment)** — Fastest to execution, lowest credibility
5. **Option 3 (Defer)** — Solves survival, postpones the problem
