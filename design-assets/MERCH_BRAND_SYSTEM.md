# indii.music Merchandise Brand System — T-Shirt Treatment

Canonical brand pairing for every merch application:

> **indii.music**
> **music business at the speed of you**

The tagline is a **primary brand asset**. It is defined once in
`packages/shared/src/brand.ts` (`INDII_BRAND.tagline`) and must always read
exactly: `music business at the speed of you` — lowercase, no period, no
"the" prefix, no paraphrase, no shortened or alternate versions.

This document defines the standard T-shirt treatment so it can later be
generated through indii.music's own merchandise capabilities (see
`packages/renderer/src/modules/merchandise/`).

---

## Front — the double ii logo

- **Garment:** white T-shirt.
- **Placement:** the logo is centered on the chest (not left-chest).
- **Mark:** the canonical **double ii** — the established indii.music icon:
  - Rounded-square outer shape.
  - Dark/black interior field (ink panel).
  - Cyan/aqua double lowercase-i symbol.
  - Colored perimeter treatment drawn from the established indii.music
    palette.
- **Canonical assets:** `design-assets/colorways/*.svg` (generated by
  `design-assets/generate-icons.mjs` — the single source of truth for the
  icon recipe; see `design-assets/ICON_COLORS.md`). **Do not redraw,
  approximate, or substitute the double ii when a canonical asset exists.**
- **Perimeter color:** any single approved solid color from the indii.music
  palette (see `ICON_COLORS.md` colorways). Different shirts/people may use
  different approved palette colors while retaining the same double ii
  identity.

## Back — the canonical tagline

- **Text (two lines, common left edge, left aligned):**

  ```
  music business
  at the speed of you
  ```

- Both lines share the same left edge; the block is positioned appropriately
  across the upper back.
- **Type:** clean black typography on the white shirt.
- **Nothing else:** no URLs, slogans, illustrations, or other competing
  elements.

## What not to add

- No additional slogans or tagline variants (including capitalized or
  period-suffixed forms).
- No redrawn logos.
- No more than one palette color on the perimeter per shirt.

---

## Brand imagery direction

Campaign imagery involving the shirts must not depict indii.music users as
one homogeneous band or one narrow music subculture. The audience is **any
independent person making music**. The visual range extends conceptually
from:

> Someone who wrote songs and wants to sing them with a guitar at a coffee
> shop

all the way to:

> An ambitious independent band or artist intending to become one of the
> biggest acts in the world.

### Casting

Campaign groups must represent a genuinely broad independent-music
population:

- Different ages — clearly young, middle-aged, older, and elderly musicians.
- Different heights and body types.
- Different socioeconomic presentations.
- Different cultural backgrounds and parts of the world.
- Different hairstyles and personal aesthetics: clean-cut artists, grungy
  DIY kids, punk/alternative artists, electronic/techno producers,
  folk/acoustic musicians, bands, solo singer-songwriters, producers,
  experimental artists, artists using culturally specific/traditional
  instruments, people who look established and financially successful, and
  people who look like they are making music with whatever resources they
  currently have.

Avoid making everyone approximately 22 years old, conventionally attractive,
fashion-coordinated, or members of the same scene.

### Direction

- The group should **not look like a band**.
- It should feel like a bunch of unrelated independent musicians from very
  different worlds were brought together and are shuffling themselves into
  position for a photograph.
- Some people face the camera. Some talk to each other. Some look away. Some
  sit. Some stand. Some turn sideways. Some face backward.
- When someone's back is visible, their shirt reveals:

  ```
  music business
  at the speed of you
  ```

- When someone's front is visible, their shirt shows the **double ii** logo.
- The personality of the individual matters more than a perfectly coordinated
  group.
